# Indra's Pearls: working notes

*Indra's Pearls: The Vision of Felix Klein* is by David Mumford, Caroline Series, and David Wright. Cambridge University Press first published it in 2002. The central object is the limit set produced by repeatedly composing Möbius transformations, especially two-generator Schottky and Kleinian groups.

This is not a substitute for having the whole book in the repository. These notes are assembled from the publisher's table of contents and description, the public chapter summary on Wikipedia, Albert Marden's review in *Notices of the AMS*, Caroline Series's resource page, and public implementations.

## Shape of the book

The publisher's contents are:

1. **The language of symmetry** — symmetry and group actions.
2. **A delightful fiction** — complex numbers, mappings of the complex plane, and the Riemann sphere.
3. **Double spirals and Möbius maps** — Möbius transformations and their classification.
4. **The Schottky dance** — two Möbius generators, Schottky groups, and breadth-first generation of limit sets.
5. **Fractal dust and infinite words** — fractal limit sets, depth-first generation, iterated-function-system ideas, and the infinite-word viewpoint.
6. **Indra's necklace** — what happens as the generating circles touch and the limit set becomes connected.
7. **The glowing gasket** — the Apollonian gasket and its relation to the modular group.
8. **Playing with parameters** — complex parameters for Schottky groups, parabolic commutators, and the relevant deformation/Teichmüller space.
9. **Accidents will happen** — the Maskit slice and the boundary between discrete and non-discrete groups.
10. **Between the cracks** — further exploration of that boundary and degenerate groups.
11. **Crossing boundaries** — further experiments, including more generators.
12. **Epilogue** — the broader non-Euclidean and Teichmüller-theory setting.

The chapter names themselves make one point especially relevant to implementation: chapter 5 is literally called **Fractal dust and infinite words**. The pictures are not just produced by repeatedly applying an anonymous numerical function. A point in the construction has a history such as

`a b A b a ...`

where `A` may be read as `a⁻¹` and similarly for `B`. That history is a word in generators and inverse generators. See `words.md`.

## Computational viewpoint

The book deliberately joins geometry to simple algorithms. Wikipedia's chapter synopsis explicitly associates chapter 4 with breadth-first search and chapter 5 with depth-first search and iterated function systems. Marden's review describes the whole book, in effect, as an exploration of the space of two-generator Schottky groups and their degenerations.

A useful implementation decomposition is therefore:

1. represent a Möbius map, for example by a projective `2 x 2` complex matrix;
2. keep a small alphabet of generators and inverses;
3. generate reduced words without immediately following a letter by its inverse;
4. evaluate those words as composed Möbius maps;
5. use the resulting images/orbits to approximate the limit set;
6. vary the generator parameters and repeat;
7. render the evolving set.

The word-generation part is discrete and automata-like even though the maps themselves live in complex geometry. That is one reason the automata/semigroup notes in this repository are not completely foreign to the project.

## Geometry that keeps recurring

- Möbius transformations act conformally on the Riemann sphere.
- Groups generated by a small number of these transformations can be discrete or non-discrete.
- Their limit sets can be dust-like, circles or Jordan curves, necklaces, and gaskets depending on the generators and parameters.
- Conjugating a group by another Möbius transformation changes the coordinate picture without changing the abstract group dynamics; this is why apparently different configurations can be versions of the same construction.
- The Maskit slice is a parameter-space picture: rather than drawing one limit set, it organizes where qualitatively different group behavior occurs.
